The weekly shape

Two hard leg days, not four

Days 1 and 3 are the true high-output sessions: jumps and heavy lower on one, sprints and horizontal power on the other. Days 2 and 4 sit at moderate, carrying the pull-up work, while Day 5 is steady aerobic work and nothing more.

The pull-up appears twice a week with different jobs. Day 2 accumulates clean volume at roughly half effort; Day 4 is where strength actually moves. Running both as hard days is the usual way this block gets wrecked.

Days 6 and 7 are rest or volleyball, whichever the week needs.

Fresh

Cut volume hard early in the week. You are testing against week one, not against ten weeks of accumulated fatigue. Measurement consistency matters more than sophisticated equipment — use the same method you used before week one.

01

Max strict pull-ups

One set, taken honestly. The standards are what make the number mean anything:

  • Dead hang at the bottom
  • No kicking or kipping
  • Chin clearly over the bar
  • Consistent technique throughout
02

Maximum vertical jump

Same measurement method as your pre-week-one baseline, or the comparison is meaningless.
